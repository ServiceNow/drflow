# Internal Compliance Incident Response Manual

## Introduction

This manual defines HarborPoint Community Bank’s internal standards for managing suspected compliance-related incidents across lending and treasury operations. It establishes consistent governance, documentation expectations, and coordination among Compliance, Legal, HR, and business line leadership. The guidance supports timely decision-making while protecting confidential information and customer data. All employees involved in incident handling must follow these standards to ensure defensible outcomes.

## Compliance Governance and Roles

Within HarborPoint’s compliance governance model, the Compliance Officer owns the initial investigation setup and must first evaluate whether a legal hold is warranted; the decision and rationale are recorded in CCP to preserve evidentiary integrity. Once that threshold is addressed, the assigned investigator develops an investigation plan in the CCP Plan tab and ties it to the CP-101 intake record to ensure traceability and consistent oversight. The plan is expected to document the core allegations and key questions to be resolved, identify required records (e.g., transaction logs, customer communications, policy attestations, access histories) and the systems or repositories where they will be retrieved, and set an interview sequence that starts with neutral fact-gathering roles before moving to involved parties. A target completion date is established to support timely escalation and regulatory responsiveness, and any dependencies or approvals are noted for management visibility. Finally, the plan is saved as a versioned artifact in CCP and linked or attached to CP-101 so that audit and second-line review can confirm what was intended, when it was approved, and how the investigation was governed.

## Conclusion

Consistent adherence to these guidelines helps HarborPoint respond to concerns in a controlled, well-documented manner. Clear role accountability and disciplined handling of information reduce operational, legal, and reputational risk. Proper documentation and escalation practices also support regulator expectations and internal oversight. Leaders are responsible for ensuring teams are trained and apply these standards uniformly.

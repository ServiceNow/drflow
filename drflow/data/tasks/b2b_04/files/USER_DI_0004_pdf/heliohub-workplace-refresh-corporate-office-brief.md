# Corporate Office Facilities Program Briefing

## Introduction

This briefing provides corporate stakeholders with an update on planned workplace services activities across HelioFreight’s North American headquarters offices. It summarizes the scope, expected operational impacts, and coordination requirements to ensure uninterrupted support for core logistics functions. The intent is to align leaders on schedule, employee experience considerations, and compliance obligations. This document is prepared for department heads, site leads, and IT/HR partners supporting the office environment.

## HelioHub Workplace Refresh Scope

The HelioHub Workplace Refresh documentation package consolidates space inventory, asset lifecycle data, and utilization benchmarks across 3 corporate locations as of 2025-11-30. In Q4 2025, Facilities and People Ops collected 612 employee survey responses and mapped 18 departmental adjacency needs to inform long-range space standards. Current collaboration areas are averaging 64% peak utilization based on 8 weeks of sensor and reservation data, which is being used for capacity modeling. The scope materials focus on cataloging constraints (lease clauses, IT cabling routes, security zones) rather than prescribing specific physical changes.

## Operational Continuity and Site Access

Operational continuity for corporate sites is governed by the 2026 Business Continuity Playbook, last tested during the 2025-10 tabletop exercise with 47 participants from IT, Finance, and Customer Operations. Badge provisioning follows a 48-hour SLA and is tracked weekly; average completion time in January 2026 was 31 hours with 2 escalations tied to HR onboarding delays. Visitor access is managed through pre-registration, with 1,284 visitors logged in 2025 and a 0.6% exception rate requiring security escort. Site access controls emphasize predictable traffic flow and safety compliance without altering day-to-day workspace configurations.

## Vendor Management and Budget Controls

Vendor management for corporate services runs through Procurement’s preferred supplier program, with 23 active vendors and quarterly scorecards tracking on-time delivery and rework rates. For FY2026, corporate services spend is capped at $2.8M OPEX with a $750K contingency, and any single PO over $50,000 requires CFO approval and three competitive bids. Invoice accuracy is controlled via three-way match in Coupa, where Q1 2026 exception volume averaged 14 invoices per month, primarily due to mismatched service dates. Budget controls prioritize rate adherence, contract terms, and audit readiness rather than defining the underlying work being performed.

## Conclusion

The planned workplace refresh will require coordinated scheduling, clear communications, and disciplined change control to minimize disruption. Leaders should confirm departmental blackout periods, validate access and security dependencies, and nominate a single point of contact per function for issue escalation. Facilities will publish a finalized calendar, site readiness checklist, and weekly status cadence for affected locations. Approval of any scope changes should be routed through the established governance process to protect timelines and cost targets.

# Contract Redline Governance for Customer Deals

## Introduction

This document provides an internal framework for managing customer contract redlines for HelioFreight’s warehousing and freight services. It is intended to help Sales, Deal Desk, and Operations align on how legal positions, risk thresholds, and approval authorities should be applied during negotiations. The guidance is designed to reduce cycle time while maintaining consistent contractual protections across regions and customer segments. Commercial Counsel will use this framework to support faster, defensible deal decisions.

## Core Transportation Liability Guardrails

Across 2025, HelioFreight’s freight claims frequency averaged 0.18% per shipment on 9.6M line-hauls, with higher incidence in LTL lanes during winter storms. In the same period, average paid claim severity was $1,740, while the top decile exceeded $9,500 largely due to electronics and high-value components. Operations also reported that 14% of claims involved packaging nonconformance identified at pickup, prompting a refresher bulletin issued on 02/05/2026. Finance is incorporating these loss patterns into the FY26 reserve model and carrier selection scorecards.

## Approval Authorities and Documentation Standards

For audit readiness, HelioFreight retained 1,286 executed customer agreements in the 2025 repository refresh, with 97% meeting the naming convention adopted on 06/01/2025. Internal Audit noted that attachment indexing improved after the template metadata cleanup, reducing retrieval time by 22% in a February 2026 sampling exercise. Procurement and AP also aligned on a standardized vendor W-9 and ACH validation packet, completing 310 validations in H2 2025 with a 1.9% exception rate. These documentation metrics are tracked quarterly for SOX support and eDiscovery response consistency.

## Redline Intake and Risk Triage

During redline intake, we first confirm whether any scope elements would force non-standard approval routes before engaging clause-by-clause legal review. For this deal, the customer has not asked for above-standard insurance limits, and the services exclude regulated or high-risk cargo (e.g., hazmat moves or temperature-controlled pharmaceutical freight), which keeps the review on the standard 3PL guardrails. We also verified there is no customer integration work—no system/API connectivity and no handling of personal data beyond routine shipment-status notifications and ordinary billing points of contact—so InfoSec/Privacy review and addenda are not in play. With those common delay drivers ruled out up front, Legal can focus triage on the true “Red” deviations (liability, indemnity, and SLA credits) and move the package into routing without waiting on additional stakeholder queues.

## Conclusion

Applying a consistent intake and triage approach, paired with clear guardrails and documented approval authorities, will reduce avoidable negotiation loops and improve turnaround time. Stakeholders should route redlines with complete deal context, use approved fallback language where available, and escalate only true exceptions against established thresholds. Legal will maintain the standard positions and decision log to ensure repeatable outcomes and audit-ready support for pricing and risk decisions. Next steps are to socialize this framework with Sales leadership and Deal Desk, then incorporate it into the contract request intake form and playbook templates.

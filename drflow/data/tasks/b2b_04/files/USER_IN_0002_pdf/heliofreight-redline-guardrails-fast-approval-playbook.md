# Contract Redline Governance Playbook

## Introduction

This internal playbook summarizes HelioFreight’s contracting guardrails and the operating cadence for reviewing customer paper across warehousing and freight services. It is intended for Sales, Deal Desk, and Operations leaders who need predictable timelines and clear escalation paths during negotiations. The document standardizes how we intake redlines, align on risk positions, and document approvals for auditability. It also clarifies when counsel involvement is required versus when teams can proceed using pre-approved language.

## Expedited Review and Approval Workflow

The CLM platform’s March 15, 2026 release improved document comparison rendering time by 27% on files over 80 pages, based on IT’s sandbox testing. Over the last 90 days, the system maintained 99.93% uptime, with two scheduled maintenance windows totaling 4 hours. Finance noted that PO matching exceptions fell from 9.8% to 7.1% after standardizing cost-center fields in deal records. Operations also piloted an automated task reminder feature in three warehouses, reducing late status updates by 12% without changing any contractual review practices.

## Redline Intake and Risk Categorization

During redline intake, the CLM compare output was mapped to the clause playbook to separate routine edits from true risk departures. The proposed changes to the limitation of liability and indemnification framework were categorized as a “Red” deviation, which automatically triggers the Legal review track rather than a standard fallback position. To keep cycle time tight, the approval routing is being limited to three functional approvers in CLM—Legal, Finance, and Operations—aligned to commercial, margin, and service-delivery accountability. At this stage, neither Risk Management nor InfoSec is anticipated in the route unless the counterparty introduces new issues that expand the risk profile.

## Fallback Positions for Key Clauses

HelioFreight’s clause library is benchmarked annually against 14 peer 3PL contracts, with the latest survey completed on November 20, 2025 by the Legal Ops team. In that benchmarking set, 73% of providers used tiered service credits, while only 22% explicitly tied credits to invoice periods longer than 30 days. The Commercial Counsel group also tracked 19 arbitration-venue variations in 2025 and summarized them for the Board’s Audit Committee in a year-end memo. These reference materials are stored in the Legal SharePoint under “Contract Standards – NA” for general awareness and template maintenance.

## Conclusion

Using a consistent intake package, clear risk tiers, and defined approver lanes reduces cycle time and prevents avoidable escalations. Teams should adopt the expedited workflow for in-policy changes and reserve exceptions for documented business-critical needs. For recurring customer asks, update fallback positions and playbook language to minimize repetitive drafting. Commercial Counsel will monitor turnaround times and adjust thresholds as claim trends, customer requirements, and operational realities evolve.

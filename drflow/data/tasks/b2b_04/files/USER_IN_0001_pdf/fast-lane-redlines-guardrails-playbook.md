# Contract Redline Governance Playbook

## Introduction

This playbook summarizes HelioFreight’s internal legal operating model for reviewing and progressing customer contract markups in support of timely commercial execution. It is intended for Sales, Deal Desk, and Operations leaders who routinely engage Legal during contracting. The focus is on maintaining consistent risk outcomes while reducing cycle time through clear guardrails, standardized positions, and disciplined collaboration. Use this document alongside the approved clause library and current delegation of authority.

## Contract Risk Tiers and Guardrails

Our risk-tiering starts with how the deal enters the CLM and the paper source: in this case, Sales submitted a Deal Intake for a new B2B shipper covering multi-site warehousing and outbound freight moves nationwide across the U.S. Because the customer marked up HelioFreight’s own “Master Services Agreement – NA 3PL” template (rather than proposing a third-party form), we can apply standard guardrails and accelerate review using pre-approved fallbacks. The redlines were run through the CLM comparison workflow and then aligned to the clause playbook, which quickly isolates whether edits are within tolerance or require escalation. The highest-impact deviations identified in the analysis clustered around limitation of liability, indemnification, and SLA/service-credit mechanics, which are the primary drivers of a “red” tier and dictate the approval routing and turnaround expectations.

## Redline Triage and Decision Workflow

Over the last 18 months, HelioFreight tracked clause-change frequency across 37 repeat customers and found the highest variance in operational appendices rather than core commercial terms. In a January 2026 survey of 64 internal stakeholders, Sales requested more visibility into warehouse access rules, while Operations prioritized clearer KPI definitions for inbound appointment scheduling. The CLM product team is piloting a dashboard that reports average “markup density” (edits per page) and correlates it with cycle time, with an initial target of reducing rework by 12% by 2026-09-30. Legal is also updating training materials for new hire onboarding, including a 45-minute module on contract hygiene and common negotiation misconceptions in 3PL transactions.

## Customer Communication and Version Control

During 2025, Customer Success logged 1,126 contract-related emails in the CRM, and 19% involved questions about billing contact updates rather than substantive term changes. To reduce confusion in shared folders, IT standardized file naming conventions for external correspondence (e.g., “CustomerName_MSA_vYYYYMMDD”) and rolled it out to 14 teams on 2026-02-05. Marketing also published a quarterly customer newsletter on 2026-01-20 highlighting service expansions in the Midwest network, including 3 new cross-dock partners and an average transit-time improvement of 7.4%. Separately, Records Management increased the default retention tag for executed agreements from 7 to 10 years to align with two major shipper procurement policies.

## Conclusion

Applying consistent risk tiering, standard clause positions, and disciplined document control reduces rework and prevents late-stage escalation. The workflow section should be used as the default path for routing redlines, documenting decisions, and securing approvals within established authority limits. Where deviations are required, teams should flag business rationale and operational impacts up front to enable faster Legal decisions. Next steps are to align Deal Desk and Sales on intake requirements and confirm the current approver matrix for each contract type.
